What the first 30 days look like

So nothing about the early numbers comes as a surprise

Days 1–3

Review & delivery

Meta reviews every ad before it serves. Spend looks lumpy and cost per result looks high. Normal, and not a signal.

Days 4–10

Learning phase

Meta needs roughly 50 conversions per ad set before delivery stabilises. We leave campaigns alone here — editing restarts the clock and wastes the spend already invested.

Days 11–21

First real read

Now the data means something. We cut what isn’t working, push budget behind the winning style and audience, and brief the next creative batch off what earned the clicks.

Days 22–30

Scale & widen

Full inventory lands. We add the delayed styles and open Pinterest against an audience Meta has already warmed.

Why Meta first, then Pinterest, then Google

The order is the strategy, not a queue

The hook isn’t the lash. It’s the glue nobody wants to touch.

Every competitor sells the look. Pink Rabbit is one of the few that can open on the part people actually dread — the glue, the tweezers, twenty minutes at the mirror. That is the first thing in frame.

Strategy note · Brooke

Brown Sugar carries the launch on its own.

Best seller across every point of sale, so it gets its own ad set and the largest share of budget. Holding the launch for full inventory would have cost three weeks of learning for a catalogue we can widen later at no cost.

The monthly rhythm

Same four beats every month, so you always know what is coming

Week 1

Report & reset

Previous month’s report by the 5th, then a 30-minute strategy call. Budgets and targets set for the month ahead.

Week 2

Creative refresh

New creative goes live against the previous winners. Anything below benchmark after seven days is retired.

Week 3

Optimise

Audience, placement and bid work. Budget moves toward whatever is returning.

Week 4

Plan forward

Next month’s angles, promos and creative brief agreed — with runway for stock, shipping and production.
